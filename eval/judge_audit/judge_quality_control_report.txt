Judge Quality Control for the Normative Reasoning Reward Model
==============================================================

Purpose
-------

The goal of this judge quality control step was to test whether the local judge used for
rewarding normative reasoning behaves in a useful and interpretable way before using it
as a reward signal in GRPO training. In this project, the judge is not only expected to
reward the final harmful/unharmful label, but also the quality of the normative
reasoning that supports the label. This is important because a model could otherwise
receive reward for a correct label while giving shallow, misleading, or poorly grounded
reasoning.

Several audit datasets were present during development, but the final quality-control
interpretation reported here is based on:

  eval/judge_audit/real_prompts_synthetic_reasoning_v1.jsonl

This dataset was chosen because it combines realistic adversarial prompts with controlled
synthetic reasoning variants. It therefore tests the judge on prompts that resemble the
actual training/evaluation setting while still making it possible to know in advance
which reasoning variants should receive higher or lower scores.

Audit Dataset
-------------

The final dataset contains 40 examples derived from 10 real adversarial prompts. For
each prompt, four synthetic completion variants were included:

  1. perfect_articulate:
     A well-structured reasoning trace that supports the correct gold label.

  2. adequate_but_improvable:
     A mostly correct but weaker reasoning trace.

  3. articulate_wrong_label:
     A fluent and plausible-looking reasoning trace that supports the wrong final label.

  4. insufficient:
     A weak, missing, or substantially underdeveloped reasoning trace.

The prompts are adversarial by construction and cover cases where superficial framing can
hide the actual risk, for example privacy invasion, stereotyping, cyber misuse, or
requests framed as fictional, educational, or authorized. This makes the audit more
diagnostic than a random set of easy examples, because the judge has to attend to the
substantive safety-relevant reasoning rather than only surface fluency.

Judging Procedure
-----------------

The local judge under evaluation was:

  opencompass/CompassJudger-1-1.5B-Instruct

It was evaluated in two reward modes:

  1. gold_direction:
     The judge received the gold harmful/unharmful direction and was expected to reward
     reasoning that correctly supports that direction. The ideal ranking was:

       perfect_articulate > adequate_but_improvable > articulate_wrong_label > insufficient

  2. judge_plus_accuracy:
     The reward included the judge score together with label correctness. In this setup,
     an articulate completion with the wrong label can still receive some credit for
     reasoning quality, but should remain below a correct and well-reasoned answer. The
     ideal ranking used in the rank-order analysis was:

       perfect_articulate > articulate_wrong_label > adequate_but_improvable > insufficient

For each mode, the audit measured:

  - mean score by reasoning variant;
  - mean score by gold label and variant;
  - per-prompt ordering checks;
  - exact rank-order match against the expected ordering;
  - pairwise ranking accuracy across all variant pairs.

OpenAI-based judges were also scored during development as reference points. These
results were useful for comparison, but the final practical decision concerns whether the
small local CompassJudger is good enough to use as the first reward model for exploration.

Main Findings
-------------

1. CompassJudger clearly rewards the best reasoning in gold_direction mode.

In gold_direction mode, CompassJudger assigned the highest average score to
perfect_articulate reasoning:

  - perfect_articulate:        0.81
  - adequate_but_improvable:   0.26
  - articulate_wrong_label:    0.30
  - insufficient:              0.00

The strongest result is that perfect_articulate reasoning was ranked above adequate,
wrong-label, and insufficient reasoning for every prompt in the simple pairwise checks:

  - perfect >= adequate:       100%
  - adequate >= insufficient:  100%
  - perfect >= wrong-label:    100%

The full rank-order match was 60%, and pairwise ranking accuracy was 78.3%. This means
that the judge does not always reproduce the complete intended ordering, but it usually
gets the important pairwise distinctions right, especially the distinction between strong
correct reasoning and weak or incorrect reasoning.

2. The judge is conservative toward insufficient reasoning.

Across both modes, insufficient reasoning received a mean score of 0.00 from
CompassJudger. This is desirable for GRPO reward shaping, because it reduces the chance
that the training process rewards empty or superficial normative reasoning. It also
suggests that the judge is sensitive to whether a completion contains a substantive
justification rather than only the correct answer tag.

3. CompassJudger is less stable when separating reasoning quality from label correctness.

In judge_plus_accuracy mode, CompassJudger scores were:

  - perfect_articulate:        0.70
  - articulate_wrong_label:    0.51
  - adequate_but_improvable:   0.16
  - insufficient:              0.00

The exact rank-order match was 50%, and pairwise ranking accuracy was 75.8%. The judge
still gives the highest average score to the perfect variant and the lowest score to the
insufficient variant. However, it sometimes gives relatively high scores to articulate
wrong-label reasoning. This indicates that the judge values fluent and safety-relevant
reasoning even when the final label is wrong, which is useful in one sense but risky if
label correctness is not sufficiently enforced elsewhere in the reward function.

4. Performance differs between harmful and unharmful gold labels.

In gold_direction mode, perfect_articulate examples with harmful gold labels received a
very high mean score of 0.98, while perfect_articulate examples with unharmful gold
labels received a lower mean score of 0.64. This suggests that the judge may be more
confident when evaluating harmful-direction reasoning than when evaluating unharmful
reasoning. A possible explanation is that safety-relevant harmful reasoning contains more
explicit risk cues, while correct unharmful reasoning often requires recognizing the
absence of actionable harm or misuse.

5. Compared with larger reference judges, CompassJudger is less smooth but more useful
than expected for its size.

The OpenAI reference judges generally assigned very high scores to perfect and adequate
reasoning, but some reference runs also gave high scores to insufficient variants. For
example, in gold_direction mode, GPT-4o-mini scored insufficient reasoning at 0.89 on
average, and GPT-5.4-mini scored it at 0.79 on average. CompassJudger was much harsher
on insufficient reasoning, assigning it 0.00. This makes CompassJudger attractive for
reward-model exploration, because the GRPO setup benefits from a judge that strongly
penalizes missing or shallow reasoning.

At the same time, CompassJudger is not perfect. Its average score for articulate wrong
labels is not always lower than adequate reasoning, and its exact rank-order accuracy is
moderate rather than high. Therefore, it should be treated as a practical first-choice
judge for experimentation, not as a final validated evaluator.

Conclusion
----------

Overall, the audit supports using CompassJudger as the first local judge for exploring
judge-reward GRPO training. For a 1.5B-parameter judge model, it performs surprisingly
well on the most important quality-control criteria: it strongly rewards articulate
correct reasoning, reliably rejects insufficient reasoning, and usually preserves the
expected pairwise ordering between strong and weak completions.

The main limitation is that CompassJudger still confuses some finer distinctions,
especially between fluent wrong-label reasoning and weaker correct reasoning. This means
that the judge reward should not be interpreted as a perfect scientific measurement of
normative reasoning quality. Instead, it is a compact, local, and reasonably aligned
reward signal that is good enough for initial GRPO experiments, provided that results are
monitored with separate evaluation and manual inspection.

Future Improvements
-------------------

Future work could improve the judge quality-control setup in several ways:

  1. Expand the audit set beyond 10 prompts while keeping the controlled four-variant
     structure. This would make the rank-order statistics more reliable.

  2. Add more unharmful examples, because the current results suggest that CompassJudger
     may be less confident when rewarding correct unharmful reasoning.

  3. Separate reasoning quality and label correctness more explicitly in the reward
     design. This would reduce the risk that fluent but wrong-label completions receive
     too much reward.

  4. Calibrate CompassJudger scores against a small manually annotated validation set,
     rather than relying only on synthetic variant labels.

  5. Add disagreement analysis between CompassJudger and stronger reference judges to
     identify systematic failure cases, such as over-rewarding fluent but incorrect
     rationales or under-rewarding concise but sufficient reasoning.

  6. Periodically re-run this audit after reward-function changes, model checkpoint
     changes, or prompt-template changes, so that the reward signal remains stable across
     training iterations.

In summary, CompassJudger is not a final gold-standard judge, but the audit results show
that it is a strong and practical first choice for local experimentation with judge-based
GRPO reward training.
